Sports business · MLB labour talks, November 2026

Should baseball have a salary cap?

MLB’s labour deal expires on December 1. The league has offered a hard cap of $258.2 million and a floor of $180.2 million from 2028, and the players’ union says it will never accept a cap. This page tests the claims both sides make, with 27 seasons of payrolls and four leagues of standings, and lets you set the cap yourself.

03The evidence from capped leaguesMLB · NBA · NFL · NHL

Do caps make leagues more even?

The NBA, NFL and NHL all limit payrolls. If a cap produces balance, those leagues should look more even than baseball. Two measures: how spread out the standings are within a season, and how much last season’s record carries into the next.

04A different designThe Premier League model

What if the limit followed each team’s revenue?

English football does not use one cap for every club. From 2026–27 the Premier League limits each club’s squad costs to 85% of its own revenue. Applied to baseball, every team would have to keep its payroll between a minimum and a maximum share of what it earns. The Premier League also debated an anchor, which stops any club spending more than a set multiple of what the lowest-earning club takes in. All three settings are below.

    05VerdictWhat the evidence supports

    A cap alone is unproven. The floor does more of the work.

    Money matters in baseball, so the owners have a real problem to point to. The evidence that a cap would fix it is weaker: capped leagues are not reliably more even, and under the league’s own offer the floor moves more teams and more money than the cap. This reading does not cover the share of revenue players receive, which is the part of the offer that matters most to them.

            Where the numbers come from

            • Payrolls: Cot’s Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus), 2000 to 2026, retrieved 11 November 2026. Year-end 40-man payrolls are used for history. The simulator uses projected 2026 luxury-tax payrolls.
            • MLB records, playoff teams and champions: MLB Stats API, 2000 to 2026.
            • NBA: NBA Stats standings, 1999–00 to 2025–26. NHL: NHL Stats API, 1995–96 to 2025–26. NFL: nflverse game results, 1999 to 2025.
            • The league’s offer: Associated Press report of 4 November 2026.
            • Team revenues: Forbes estimates for the 2025 season, published 20 March 2026. They are net of revenue sharing and exclude playoff revenue. Premier League rule: Premier League statement of 21 November 2025.

            What to keep in mind

            • The simulator is static. It applies the rule to 2026 payrolls and assumes nothing else changes. Real teams would restructure contracts, and the league’s offer lets a team spend 90% of the floor in a given year.
            • Correlation is not cause. Payroll and wins move together, but winning teams also earn more and then spend more. The estimated win changes are what the past link implies, and they are not forecasts.
            • Leagues differ in more than their cap. Roster size, the draft, season length and the role of single stars all affect balance. The NBA comparison shows that a cap does not guarantee balance, and it does not show that a cap has no effect.
            • Payroll figures are unofficial. They are compiled from published reports, and the 2026 values are projections.
            • Revenues are estimates from a year earlier. Teams do not publish their accounts, so the revenue section sets 2026 payrolls against Forbes’s 2025 revenue estimates. The shares are approximate.
