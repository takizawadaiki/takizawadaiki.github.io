Where the numbers come from

  • Income: Statistics Canada, table 11-10-0239-01, median total income of people aged 25 to 34, 1976 to 2024.
  • Prices: Statistics Canada Consumer Price Index, annual average, table 18-10-0005-01. All dollar figures are in 2025 dollars.
  • House prices: Bank for International Settlements, residential property prices for Canada, via FRED (series QCAN628BIS).
  • Mortgage rate: CMHC five-year conventional mortgage lending rate, Statistics Canada table 34-10-0145-01.
  • Rent: CMHC average rent for one-bedroom units in apartment buildings of six units and over, Statistics Canada table 34-10-0133-01.
  • Tuition: Statistics Canada tables 37-10-0150-01 and 37-10-0003-01. Jobs: Labour Force Survey, table 14-10-0327-01.

What to keep in mind

  • These are national medians and averages. They describe a typical person, and nobody is exactly typical. Results differ a lot by city, education and family background.
  • Income is before tax and per person. More 25 to 34 year olds are in school now than in 1976, and far more women work full time, which changes who is counted in the median.
  • The housing lines are indexes. They show how prices and payments moved against income, and they do not give a price in dollars. The payment line ignores the down payment, which is harder to save when prices are high.
  • CMHC rents cover older purpose-built apartments and include long-term tenants, so they sit below what a new renter is asked to pay today.
  • Six measures are not a life. Health, safety, technology, travel and choice have changed too, mostly for the better, and are not counted here.
