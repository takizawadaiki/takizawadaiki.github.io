What happened to grocery prices?

Statistics Canada records the shelf price of 110 everyday products each month in every province. This page compares what they cost in 2019 with what they cost now, sets that against wages, and lets you price your own basket.

    What to keep in mind

    • Shelf prices are averages. Each figure is the average price across stores and brands for a product of that size. If shoppers move to cheaper brands, or the mix of stores changes, the average moves too. The consumer price index is the official measure of price change, and it is shown beside the shelf prices for that reason.
    • An average wage is not everyone’s wage. The average hourly wage also shifts when the mix of jobs changes. Someone whose pay did not rise fell further behind than these figures show, and the comparison leaves out taxes, rent and everything else a paycheque covers.
    • The basket is an example. The typical basket is my own list of common items, not an official one. Change it to match what you buy.
    • Each product is tracked at one size. The price is for the package size named, such as 500 grams of pasta.
    • 2019 is the starting point. It is the last full year before the pandemic. A different starting year would give different figures.
    • Provinces only. There are no figures by city, and the territories have too few products to include.
