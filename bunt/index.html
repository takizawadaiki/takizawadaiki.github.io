What is a sacrifice bunt really worth?

Japanese teams bunt a runner over several times as often as MLB teams. This page measures what the play is worth, using every MLB plate appearance since 2015 and a run-scoring model built for both leagues, and asks whether Japan’s lower-scoring game justifies the difference.

      What to keep in mind

      • Teams choose when to bunt. Real bunts happen when the defence is back, the runner is fast or the hitter is good at it. The comparison with swings matches the situation and the class of hitter, but it cannot match everything a manager sees.
      • Japan’s results are modelled. There is no public play-by-play for NPB. The model reproduces each league’s scoring from its totals, and it assumes runners advance on hits at MLB rates in both leagues.
      • Bunt skill may differ. Japanese hitters practise the sacrifice far more, so their success rate is probably higher, and they bunt for hits less. Use the success slider in the tool to see how much that matters.
      • The win-probability figures are simple. They assume both teams score at the league-average rate in every later inning and treat a tie after nine as a coin flip. They ignore the bullpen, the next hitters in the order and the extra-inning runner rule.
      • The hitter is described by one number. Speed, bunting skill, the pitcher and the defence all matter and are not modelled.
