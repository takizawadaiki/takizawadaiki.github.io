Shots, offence or defence: what wins in the NHL?

Two questions hockey fans argue about. Does putting more shots on goal than the other team win games? And is it the high-scoring teams or the stingy ones that win the Stanley Cup? This page tests both.

    What to keep in mind

    • A shot on goal is not a scoring chance. The count treats a point shot through traffic and a breakaway the same. Shot quality is not in this data.
    • The fair test is narrow. Scoreless first periods are about a fifth of games, and one period is a small sample of shots, so that estimate is noisy.
    • There are few champions. One team wins each year. With 46 champions, the gap between offence and defence would have to be large to show up clearly, and it is not.
    • Regular-season numbers describe playoff teams. Offence and defence are measured over the regular season. Trades, injuries and a hot goalie in the spring are not captured.
    • Goals allowed is not only defence. It mixes the skaters’ defending with goaltending, and a team that has the puck a lot allows fewer goals too.
    • Eras differ. Scores are measured against the league in the same season so that the high-scoring 1980s and the low-scoring 2000s can be compared.
