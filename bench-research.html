THE RESEARCH, IN BRIEF

Teams that distribute more minutes to bench players during the regular season tend to get more bench production in the playoffs. Across three NBA seasons (2023–26), this relationship is statistically significant — but the outliers reveal that minutes alone don't tell the full story.

r 0.300 Correlation Bench min (reg) → Bench pts (playoffs)
p 0.038 P-value Significant at α = 0.05
48 Observations 16 playoff teams × 3 seasons

01 /Motivation

Every NBA front office talks about depth. Load management, roster construction, the value of a reliable second unit — these are constant topics in team-building conversations. But the empirical question is rarely asked directly: does the bench investment you make in the regular season actually show up when it matters most?

Playoff basketball is famously rotation-heavy. Coaches tighten their rotations, star players log heavy minutes, and bench contributions shrink. If that's true, maybe regular season bench usage is a poor predictor of playoff bench output. Or maybe teams that develop a functional bench throughout the year carry that advantage into the postseason. This research tests which is closer to the truth.

02 /Data & Methodology

Data was pulled from the NBA Stats API (nba_api) covering the 2023–24, 2024–25, and 2025–26 NBA seasons. For each season, bench player stats were aggregated at the team level for both the regular season and playoffs using the starter_bench_nullable='Bench' filter.

Key variables:

bench_min — average minutes per game per bench player, regular season

playoff_bench_pts — average points per game per bench player, playoffs

Sample: 16 playoff teams per season × 3 seasons = 48 observations

Test: Pearson correlation with two-tailed p-value (scipy.stats)

Using average per-player minutes (rather than total bench minutes) controls for roster size differences between teams. A team with 20 bench players averaging 10 minutes is meaningfully different from one with 12 bench players averaging 16 minutes — the latter reflects genuine depth investment.

03 /Results

Across 48 team-season observations, the correlation between regular season bench minutes and playoff bench scoring is r = 0.300 (p = 0.038) — a moderate positive relationship that clears the conventional significance threshold.

The positive trend is consistent across all three seasons, though the effect varies year to year. The 2024–25 season showed the strongest individual-season correlation (r = 0.316), while 2025–26 showed the weakest (r = 0.156), suggesting external factors — injuries, coaching decisions, opponent matchups — add meaningful noise to any single season.

05 /Discussion

The finding supports the intuition that bench development has carry-over value into the playoffs, but the effect is modest. An r of 0.300 means bench minute investment explains roughly 9% of the variance in playoff bench output — real, but far from deterministic.

The outliers suggest two important nuances. First, role clarity may matter more than volume. Minnesota's bench players logged few regular season minutes but performed at a high level when deployed in the playoffs — implying that knowing your role and executing it under pressure is as valuable as simply accumulating minutes. Second, star-dependency creates fragility. The LAL collapse is consistent with a roster where bench players were never truly integrated into the offensive system — they existed to rest the stars, not to function independently.

For front offices, this suggests that bench investment should be evaluated not just by minutes distributed but by whether bench players are being developed in meaningful, system-integrated roles during the regular season.

Key Takeaway

Across three NBA seasons, teams that gave their bench more regular season minutes tended to get meaningfully better bench production in the playoffs (r = 0.300, p = 0.038). The relationship is statistically significant but explains only ~9% of variance — suggesting bench quality, role clarity, and system integration are equally important factors that minutes alone don't capture.

Future work could incorporate bench net rating (rather than scoring) and control for opponent quality to build a more complete picture of what drives playoff bench performance.

06 /Data & Code

Data sourced from the NBA Stats API via nba_api (Python). Analysis conducted in Google Colab using pandas, scipy, and matplotlib. All three seasons (2023–24, 2024–25, 2025–26) were pulled using the LeagueDashPlayerStats endpoint with starter_bench_nullable='Bench'.
